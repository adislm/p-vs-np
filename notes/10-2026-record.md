# The 2026 record

A verified snapshot of what actually moved in complexity theory in 2026,
compiled so that later claims can be checked against a fixed baseline. Every
arXiv identifier below was resolved against the arXiv API at the time of
writing; a resolved identifier confirms the paper exists, not that its claims are
correct.

The year is crowded with AI-generated and self-posted claims, so status labels
are doing real work here.

## Peer-reviewed

**Chen, Tal and Wang, "Super-quadratic Lower Bounds for Depth-2 Linear Threshold
Circuits", STOC 2026** (ECCC TR26-039, March 2026). For every ε > 0 there is a
function in E^NP requiring THR∘THR circuits — and SYM∘THR circuits — of size
n^{2.5−ε}. This is the first super-quadratic depth-2 threshold lower bound in
about a decade, obtained through Williams' algorithmic method and CAPP.

It is a genuine advance and it is a restricted-class result: depth-2 threshold
circuits, one function, in a class above NP. It does not touch P vs NP.

| Lecomte and Ramakrishnan, "Optimal Shallow Circuits for Majority": depth-d circuits of size 2^{O(n^{1/(d−1)})} for all symmetric functions, matching Håstad's lower bound | arXiv:2609.34029 (27 Sep 2026) | preprint, and an **upper**-bound construction |

**Read the Chukhin et al. result carefully, because the headline is easy to
misread.** It is a win–win dichotomy in the Kabanets–Impagliazzo style, not an
unconditional lower bound for NP. The disjunction is between a strong
derandomisation consequence and an algebraic separation; neither horn is
established, and the paper does not claim otherwise. Anyone quoting it as
"unconditional circuit lower bounds" for NP is quoting the title rather than the
statement.

**The Lecomte–Ramakrishnan paper is worth a note for a different reason.** It is
an upper-bound result settling the asymptotic AC⁰ complexity of Majority, and its
abstract credits an AI model. That is the shape of AI's genuine contributions in
this area so far: construction, search, and formalisation, in settings where the
target is checkable. Not discovery of lower bounds.

## Unreviewed and contested

**A claimed deterministic 2^{o(n)} 3-SAT algorithm, reported in late October
2026, presented as refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis.** No primary
manuscript was verified; what resolves is commentary, notably
arXiv:2611.00517, "Limits and Constants of OpenAI's Claimed ETH Refutation"
(31 Oct 2026). Reported Lean formalisations are said to establish a weaker bound
than the headline, there is no expert verdict, and any practical speedup would
appear only at astronomically large n.

Two things follow even if the claim is vindicated. First, refuting ETH is a
fine-grained hardness result, not a resolution of P vs NP — the derived circuit
bound is explicitly not for NP. Second, the status as of this record is
*unreviewed*, and it is recorded here as such.

Self-posted items (a Zenodo "3-SAT linear circuit lower bound — a step towards
P ≠ NP", a claimed Unique Games proof) are unreviewed claims with no verified
primary source.

## Did anything change P vs NP, or the barriers?

**No.** Nothing verified in 2026 changes the status of P vs NP or breaches
relativisation, natural proofs or algebrization. The verified results are a
restricted depth-2 lower bound, a conditional win–win dichotomy, lower bounds for
E^NP and E^{prMA} rather than NP, and upper-bound constructions. All of them work
inside existing frameworks — the algorithmic method, Kabanets–Impagliazzo, range
avoidance — and all leave the three barriers standing.

The genuine movement is incremental: quadratic to n^{2.5} for depth-2 threshold
circuits, and win–win lower bounds that trade a derandomisation hypothesis against
an algebraic separation. That is real progress in technique. It is not progress
on the question, and the distinction is the whole point of this file.
